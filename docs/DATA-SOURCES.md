# Phone sensors label half a week, at best

As of November 2026, no automatic source can stand in for LifeTime's hand-typed calendar. Together, the phone's local sources can put a time boundary around most of a week, but they can name what happened in only about half of its hours, and only on a well-equipped phone. A worked example of an office worker (assumed hours, not measured data) shows the spread. An iPhone with an Apple Watch confidently labels about **57% of the 168 hours** from HealthKit sleep and workouts plus Core Location visits. An Android phone with a wearable that writes to Health Connect reaches **41%** from sleep, workouts and exact per-app usage, and about **65%** only once background location is added. A phone with no wearable falls to **8–24%**. The labels land in three or four of LifeTime's eight categories: Rest, Exercise, Work at a named place, and phone Entertainment on Android. **Nutrition, Social, Self-care and Chores stay almost invisible**, so the calendar, or one-tap manual capture, remains the only way to describe the social and domestic half of a week. The two platforms are mirror images. Android exposes exact app foreground intervals for ten days but has no visit API. iOS exposes visits, a seven-day motion history and rich HealthKit intervals, but keeps per-app Screen Time data in a sandbox everywhere except the EU, and even there only since iOS 26.4. Cloud adds little that is both broad and serverless: only Google Calendar and Microsoft Graph qualify, mostly for the web demo, because the wearable clouds have closed, need a server or, in Oura's case, sit behind a paid membership. Categorisation already works on-device on iPhone 15 Pro and later through Apple's Foundation Models, is beta-grade on Gemini Nano flagships, and needs a keyword-and-embedding fallback on every other device. The binding constraints are policy, not code. Apple limits HealthKit and Core Motion to visible health and fitness purposes and Screen Time to device-management apps, and Google gates Health Connect and background location behind Play declarations. On the build side, adopting the UIScene life cycle, through Expo SDK 58 or an SDK 57 backport, comes before any of this can be built with Xcode 27.

A paragraph that opens with **Inference** is reasoning drawn from those facts, not a finding. **[snippet]** after a citation means the claim rests only on a search-engine extract, because the official page could not be reached from the research sessions. "Anecdotal" marks forum posts and store reviews.

## iOS offers rich health and place intervals, but locks away app usage

On iOS, five sources produce real start-and-end intervals: HealthKit (sleep, workouts, mindfulness), Core Location visits, Core Motion's activity history, Focus filters and Shortcuts automations. Screen Time, which the owner might expect to matter most, gives intervals to no app at all.

### HealthKit turns sleep and workouts into labelled intervals

Four HealthKit families can be used almost directly as time entries. An `HKWorkout` is a sample with a start, an end and a mandatory `HKWorkoutActivityType` taken from an enum of about 85 entries. It also carries sub-activities, such as triathlon legs, and pause and resume events that separate active time from paused time ([Apple: HKWorkout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkout); [Apple: HKWorkoutActivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutactivity); [Apple: HKWorkoutEvent](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutevent)). `sleepAnalysis` stores in-bed samples plus stage samples (core, deep, REM, awake) that "overlap the in-bed sample, but they don't overlap each other" ([Apple: HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysis](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategoryvaluesleepanalysis)). `mindfulSession` is a bare interval ([Apple: mindfulSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier/mindfulsession)). The Watch's activity minutes (`appleExerciseTime`, stand time, move time and `timeInDaylight`) are different: they are **cumulative quantities over short windows, not labelled episodes** ([Apple: appleExerciseTime](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/appleexercisetime); [Apple: timeInDaylight](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/timeindaylight)).

State of Mind carries a single `date` rather than an interval ([Apple: HKStateOfMind](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkstateofmind)). Sleep stages need an Apple Watch, and an iPhone alone records only time in bed ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/tech/how-to-track-your-sleep-using-your-iphone-ipad-and-apple-watch/), secondary). There is **no HealthKit type for the Sleep Score, the sleep schedule, bedtime or Wind Down** ([Apple: HKCategoryTypeIdentifier](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategorytypeidentifier)). Since iOS 26, workout sessions can be recorded on the iPhone as well as on the Watch ([Apple: iOS 26 release notes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/ios-ipados-release-notes/ios-ipados-26-release-notes)).

Read permission is granted per type and is deliberately opaque: "an app isn't aware when the user denies permission to read data. From the app's point of view, no data of that type exists" ([Apple: Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)). **iOS 27 added a second permission screen** on which users choose "a recent limited window or their full history" ([Apple: Authorizing access to health data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)). The new `earliestAuthorizedSampleDate(for:)` reports only the limited case, and Apple advises treating data before that date "as unknown — not an absence of data" ([Apple: getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/getearliestauthorizedsampledate(for:completion:))). Apple does not say how long the window is. "Past 30 Days and Future Data" comes from one developer's forum post that has no Apple reply ([Apple Developer Forums 842499](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/842499), anecdotal).

Background sync combines observer queries with `enableBackgroundDelivery`. That call has needed a dedicated entitlement since iOS 15, wakes the app at most once per chosen frequency, and stops delivering after three unanswered wakes ([Apple: enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))). An `HKAnchoredObjectQuery` then fetches only what changed ([Apple: HKAnchoredObjectQuery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkanchoredobjectquery)). The store is encrypted while the device is locked, so background reads can fail ([Apple: Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)).

Policy is the real gate. In the App Review Guidelines (updated 16 October 2026), Guideline 2.5.1 says "HealthKit should be used for health and fitness purposes and integrate with the Health app". Guideline 5.1.3 bars using health or "Motion and Fitness" data for advertising or data mining, and forbids storing personal health information in iCloud ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The developer agreement adds that HealthKit and the Motion & Fitness APIs may be used only "for health, motion, and/or fitness purposes", and that "such uses must be clearly evident in Your marketing text and user interface" ([Apple DPLA 3.3.3(H)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)).

The implementation path is mature. **`@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit` 16.2.0** (12 October 2026, about 607k weekly downloads) ships an Expo config plugin with a background-delivery option and runs on Nitro modules ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit)). `react-native-health` has not shipped since October 2024 ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/react-native-health)). Expo has no first-party health module ([expo 58.0.23 bundledNativeModules](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo/-/expo-58.0.23.tgz)).

**Inference.** HealthKit is LifeTime's highest-value iOS source because it alone delivers both an exact boundary and a label with nothing to classify: Rest from sleep, Exercise from workouts, Self-care from mindfulness. Its cost is positioning. A "fill my timesheet" pitch is a plausible rejection under 2.5.1 and DPLA 3.3.3(H). A "sleep and exercise balance of your week" feature, visible in the UI and in store copy, fits the wording.

The opaque permissions impose two design rules. LifeTime must never show an empty result as "no exercise"; it should say "no workouts found — check Health access" instead. And on iOS 27, onboarding should plan for a possibly short backfill and label the weeks before the boundary as unknown. Because the Health app itself resolves overlapping sleep sources with a priority order ([Apple Support 108906](https://support.apple.com/en-kz/108906) [snippet]), LifeTime should pick one sleep source per night rather than merge them all. If LifeTime ever syncs its timeline through iCloud, whether a derived "Sleep 23:40–07:15" block counts as personal health information under 5.1.3(ii) is a question for App Review, not an engineering one.

### Visits and motion rebuild places and commutes, approximately

**Visits.** `startMonitoringVisits()` delivers `CLVisit` objects with a coordinate, an accuracy and approximate `arrivalDate` and `departureDate` values. A visit can arrive with only an arrival, and the departure follows later ([Apple: CLVisit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clvisit)). Apple calls visits "the most power-efficient way to get location data", delivered "at a later time" ([Apple: Getting the current location of a device](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/getting-the-current-location-of-a-device)).

The service relaunches a terminated app only under Always authorization; with When In Use, "the user must launch the app" ([Apple: Requesting authorization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/requesting-authorization-to-use-location-services)). It still uses the legacy delegate API, which an Apple engineer confirmed in June 2026 ([Apple Developer Forums 791470](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/791470)). Apple publishes no latency, accuracy or minimum-dwell figures, and one developer reports that no visit fires if monitoring starts while the user is already at a place ([Apple Developer Forums 776422](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/776422), anecdotal).

More seriously, **since iOS 26.0 some devices have silently stopped receiving visits until the app is reinstalled**. An Apple engineer called it "a regression", and the thread shows no fix as of November 2026 ([Apple Developer Forums 807301](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807301)).

Two other services complement visits. `CLMonitor` geofences are capped at **20 conditions per app** ([Apple: Monitoring the user's proximity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/monitoring-the-user-s-proximity-to-geographic-regions)). Significant-change updates arrive after a move of 500 m and no more often than every five minutes ([Apple: startMonitoringSignificantLocationChanges](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/cllocationmanager/startmonitoringsignificantlocationchanges())). Core Location changed no APIs in iOS 26 or 27 ([Apple: Core Location updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/corelocation)), and no public API exposes the system's Significant Locations.

**Motion.** Core Motion keeps only "the last seven days worth of activity data". Each record is a transition (stationary, walking, running, cycling, automotive or unknown) with a low, medium or high confidence. Apps query the history after the fact, and "a delay of up to several minutes" is normal ([Apple: queryActivityStarting](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionactivitymanager/queryactivitystarting(from:to:to:withhandler:)); [Apple: CMMotionActivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionactivity)). The flags are not mutually exclusive: a car at a red light is both automotive and stationary. Each record holds only a start date, and live updates stop while the app is suspended ([Apple: startActivityUpdates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionactivitymanager/startactivityupdates(to:withhandler:))). Core Motion needs **only the Motion & Fitness permission, not location**, and the pedometer keeps the same seven days ([Apple: queryPedometerData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/querypedometerdata(from:to:withhandler:))).

**Policy and Expo.** The two services sit under different clauses of the developer agreement. Always location must come with "a clearly defined justification and user benefit" at prompt time ([Apple DPLA 3.3.3(F)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Core Motion sits under the same health-and-fitness purpose clause as HealthKit ([Apple DPLA 3.3.3(H)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)).

The Expo path has gaps. `expo-location` 58.0.20 covers geofencing and significant change but **contains no CLVisit code at all** ([expo-location 58.0.20 tarball](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-location/-/expo-location-58.0.20.tgz)). `expo-sensors` exposes step counts but not motion activity ([npm: expo-sensors](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-sensors)). The only Expo-shaped motion module, `expo-activity-motion` 1.2.3, is weeks old and has about 36 weekly downloads ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-activity-motion)).

**Inference.** Once the user has named a place, visits map directly onto "Office 09:02–17:45", and that single naming step turns the office hours into Work. They should still be suggestions for the user to confirm, because the edges are approximate and the iOS 26 regression can stop delivery silently. Significant-change updates that keep arriving while visits do not are a cheap signal of that failure, and the moment to prompt the user.

Core Motion is the low-friction first ask. It shows no location prompt, and on install day it already holds the last week's walks, rides and drives. These become intervals by taking each transition's start as the end of the previous record and dropping low-confidence records. Trains and buses have no class and will probably appear as automotive or unknown, which is unverified and needs field testing. LifeTime has to open or wake at least once a week, or that history is lost.

Each of the two is a custom Expo module of roughly a day's work. Visits reveal home and workplace by construction, so they should be matched against places the user has named and kept only on the device.

### Screen Time hands apps a picture, not data, except in the EU

DeviceActivity reports render inside a sandboxed extension that "prevents your extension from making network requests or moving sensitive content outside the extension's address space" ([Apple: DeviceActivityReport](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity/deviceactivityreport)). An Apple DTS engineer restated in August 2026 that apps cannot see "exactly how much time they spend on each specific app" ([Apple Developer Forums 843002](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/843002)). In July 2026, a DTS engineer also said no hook fires when another app launches ([Apple Developer Forums 838231](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/838231)). Even inside the extension the data is coarse. A per-app `ApplicationActivity` holds total duration, pickups and notifications but **no timestamps**, and the finest segment is one hour ([Apple: ApplicationActivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity/deviceactivitydata/applicationactivity); [Apple: SegmentInterval](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity/deviceactivityfilter/segmentinterval-swift.enum)).

iOS 26.4 opened one exception. With the new `com.apple.developer.family-controls.app-and-website-usage` entitlement and the user-granted `approvedWithDataAccess` status, the main app can call `DeviceActivityData.activityData(filteredBy:using:)` and receive real bundle identifiers and hourly per-app durations. Two conditions apply. The first is geographic: "customer installations… can only use the method on devices located in the EU that are signed in with an Apple Account with an EU country or region" ([Apple: activityData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity/deviceactivitydata/activitydata(filteredby:using:))). The second is exclusivity: "only one app at a time can hold this authorization status on a given device" ([Apple: approvedWithDataAccess](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/familycontrols/authorizationstatus/approvedwithdataaccess)). Developers report the status falling back to `.approved` in TestFlight and App Store builds, and no route at all outside the EU, with no answer from Apple ([Device Activity forum tag](https://developer.apple.com/forums/tags/device-activity); [Apple Developer Forums 856132](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/856132), anecdotal). WWDC26 and iOS 27 changed nothing in these frameworks ([Apple: Updates index](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates)).

The worldwide workaround uses the monitor extension, which can write to the App Group. It sets a ladder of threshold events ("Social reached 5, 10, 15 minutes") and logs each one with a timestamp. `react-native-device-activity` 0.6.1 ships this pattern for Expo, and warns that "the Screen Time APIs are known to be very finnicky" ([GitHub: react-native-device-activity](https://github.com/kingstinct/react-native-device-activity)). The pattern has hard limits. An app can monitor at most 20 activities, with a 15-minute minimum interval ([Apple: excessiveActivities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity/deviceactivitycenter/monitoringerror/excessiveactivities); [Apple: intervalTooShort](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity/deviceactivitycenter/monitoringerror/intervaltooshort)). The extension's 6 MB memory cap causes crashes ([Apple Developer Forums 823431](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/823431)). On iOS 26, thresholds can fire with zero use, which DTS called "a known issue" in January 2026 ([Apple Developer Forums 809410](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/809410)).

The app and every Screen Time extension need Apple's **Family Controls (Distribution) approval, one request per bundle ID**, before any TestFlight build ([Apple: Requesting the Family Controls entitlement](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/familycontrols/requesting-the-family-controls-entitlement)). The library's README adds that until then "you can't even build an Expo Dev Client" ([GitHub: react-native-device-activity](https://github.com/kingstinct/react-native-device-activity)). Requests have sat in "Submitted" for 20 days ([Apple Developer Forums 825999](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825999), anecdotal).

The developer agreement reserves the framework for apps whose "primary purpose" is family controls or "personal device usage management", and usage data may not leave "the individual and their device" ([Apple DPLA 3.3.3(P)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)). Guideline 4.10 forbids monetising Screen Time APIs ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

**Inference.** An automatic "Instagram 21:03–21:40" line is impossible on iOS, for anyone. Outside the EU, LifeTime has two weak options. It can embed a display-only Screen Time panel that it cannot add to its totals. Or it can run a threshold ladder that estimates usage in five-minute steps, labelled "estimated" and sanity-checked against the misfire bug.

In the EU, which is plausibly where LifeTime's first users are, the iOS 26.4 path delivers per-app minutes per hour as real data. It is fragile, though: when any other app takes data access, a focus blocker for instance, LifeTime is silently reset. Positioning the feature as personal device-usage management, with optional limits or focus sessions, strengthens the "primary purpose" case, and keeping it free satisfies 4.10. The entitlement wait is long enough that the request should be filed months before the feature is needed.

### Focus filters and Shortcuts recipes trade setup for exact intervals

**Focus filters.** A Focus filter is the one supported way for an app to learn when a specific Focus starts and stops. The system runs the app's `SetFocusFilterIntent.perform()` when the Focus turns on, and again with default parameters when it turns off ([Apple: Focus](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/focus); [Apple: Defining your app's Focus filter](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/defining-your-app-s-focus-filter)). This works only for Focuses to which the user has added LifeTime's filter in Settings. `INFocusStatusCenter` reveals only a Boolean `isFocused`, meant for communication apps ([Apple: isFocused](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/intents/infocusstatus/isfocused-8ytve)).

**Shortcuts automations.** A Shortcuts personal automation can call a LifeTime App Intent on triggers such as an app being opened or closed, arriving or leaving, Wi-Fi, CarPlay or a charger connecting, Sleep wind-down, bedtime and wake-up, and a time of day ([Apple Shortcuts User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/apdfbdbd7123/) [snippet]). Since iOS 17, nearly all triggers can "Run Immediately". Sources disagree on whether the "Notify When Run" banner that comes with them can be suppressed ([Matthew Cassinelli](https://matthewcassinelli.com/automations-run-immediately-shortcuts-notifications/) [snippet]). iOS 27 moved automations into the shortcut editor and added screenshot, keyboard and notification triggers ([WWDC26 session 310](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/310/)).

**App Intents.** Intents can run in the background via `supportedModes` (iOS 26) ([Apple: supportedModes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appintents/appintent/supportedmodes)), and iOS 27 adds `LongRunningIntent` and `UndoableIntent` ([Apple: App Intents updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/appintents)). DTS stresses that Shortcuts automation is "solely for the user's use", not an API apps can drive ([Apple Developer Forums 838231](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/838231)). For one-tap manual capture, Control Center controls and Live Activities run on the same intents, and a Live Activity stays active for up to eight hours ([Apple: Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/activitykit/displaying-live-data-with-live-activities)). Expo's official **`expo-app-intents` 0.5.16** compiles intents into the app target through inline modules ([expo-app-intents 0.5.16 README](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-app-intents/-/expo-app-intents-0.5.16.tgz)).

**Inference.** These are the only worldwide iOS routes to exact app-level intervals. A recipe pair, "Instagram is opened → LifeTime Start (Instagram)" and "is closed → Stop", produces real start and stop timestamps without Screen Time. The setup cost is honest. No API creates automations, so the user builds each one, though an importable shortcut link helps. Each run may post a notification, automations exist on one device only, and the user can disable them without LifeTime knowing. That suits a handful of high-value signals, such as a Work Focus, CarPlay as a commute, or a sleep wake-up, rather than every app. A Focus filter whose parameter is a LifeTime category turns each Focus into an automatic label. Whether `perform()` fires reliably for scheduled or synced Focus changes while the phone is locked is undocumented and needs device testing.

### Smaller iOS signals fill gaps but cannot carry a week

Several minor signals are readable. Reading calendars needs full access: write-only access "can't access any of the existing calendars and events on the device, including events your app created" ([Apple: Accessing the event store](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/eventkit/accessing-the-event-store)). Completed reminders expose a `completionDate`, a point in time ([Apple: predicateForCompletedReminders](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/eventkit/ekeventstore/predicateforcompletedreminders(withcompletiondatestarting:ending:calendars:))), and photos carry a `creationDate` and a location ([Apple: PHAsset.creationDate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/photos/phasset/creationdate)). Journaling Suggestions hands over one user-picked suggestion with a `DateInterval`, including an `isWorkLocation` flag for the inferred workplace ([Apple: JournalingSuggestion.Location](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/journalingsuggestions/journalingsuggestion/location)). The developer agreement, however, confines it to "journaling purposes" ([Apple DPLA 3.3.3(H)](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)), and it gained nothing in iOS 27 ([Apple: Journaling Suggestions updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/journalingsuggestions)). Three richer sources are out of reach. SensorKit has visits with place categories and app usage per category, but "Apple only grants the entitlement for approved research studies" ([Apple: Configuring your project for sensor reading](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/configuring-your-project-for-sensor-reading)). MusicKit's recently-played request documents no per-play timestamps ([Apple: MusicRecentlyPlayedRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/musickit/musicrecentlyplayedrequest)). No public API reads Significant Locations, as noted above.

**Inference.** Completed reminders and photo bursts are useful corroboration: a reminder ticked at 15:42, or twenty photos at a park, confirms a block. They are points, though, not intervals.

Writing auto-detected entries into a dedicated "LifeTime" calendar would make them visible and editable in Apple Calendar, at three costs: it needs full calendar access, an iCloud-backed calendar would carry the data off the device, and LifeTime would read its own output back as hand-typed input. Journaling Suggestions fits only if LifeTime ships a genuine daily-reflection feature from which the user picks a moment to log.

## Android trades exact app usage for a missing visit API

Android 17 (API 37) has been stable since June 2026 ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-17-release-date-3639790) [snippet]). None of its behaviour changes touch usage stats, Health Connect, activity recognition or calendars ([Android 17 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/behavior-changes-17)). The changes that matter for LifeTime are in Health Connect and in Play policy.

### Health Connect mirrors HealthKit, behind a Play health declaration

Health Connect is part of the framework from Android 14, and an APK from Play on Android 9–13 ([Health Connect: Get started](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/get-started)). Its interval records include `SleepSessionRecord` with stages (awake, light, deep, REM, out of bed), `ExerciseSessionRecord` with about 60 exercise types, laps and segments, `MindfulnessSessionRecord`, and `ActivityIntensityRecord` ([Health Connect data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)).

By default an app reads "up to 30 days prior to when any permission was first granted". Older data needs `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`, and reinstalling restarts the window ([Health Connect: Read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/read-data)). Background reads need `READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND` behind a feature flag, and Google's sample polls with an hourly WorkManager job ([Health Connect: Read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/read-data)). `getChanges` gives incremental sync. Jetpack `connect-client` 1.1.0 is stable, and 1.2.0-alpha06 (26 August 2026) is the latest ([Health Connect release notes](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/health-connect)).

Publishing requires the Play Console health apps declaration, which justifies each data type against "specific, user-facing health features" ([Complete the health apps declaration](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/publish/declare-access)). Play's policy limits access "to apps with approved health, fitness, medical care, or health research core use cases" ([Play: Permissions and APIs that Access Sensitive Information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16909972?hl=en) [snippet]).

Data quality depends on who writes the data. Samsung Health can sync sleep with stages ([Samsung Developer blog](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect) [snippet]), and Garmin has reportedly written to Health Connect since mid-2025 ([ultrafit360](https://ultrafit360.com/blog/garmin-apple-health-health-connect), third party). A January 2026 Fitbit community thread, by contrast, reports broken syncing ([Fitbit Community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Personal-Health-Coach-Public-Preview/Fitbit-Public-Preview-Health-Connect-Syncing-Broken/td-p/5808716), anecdotal), and Google Fit's APIs end with 2026 ([Fit migration guide](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/migration/fit) [snippet]). The Expo path is **`react-native-health-connect` 4.1.3** (6 August 2026). It now bundles its Expo plugin, needs `compileSdkVersion` 36, and has open bugs on time-zone bucketing and on the history permission never being reported as granted ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/react-native-health-connect); [GitHub issues](https://github.com/matinzd/react-native-health-connect/issues)).

**Inference.** The HealthKit rules carry over: frame the reads as sleep and activity health features, request only READ_SLEEP, READ_EXERCISE and READ_MINDFULNESS, and skip the history permission in v1, because a weekly report needs only 7–30 days. Sessions should be deduplicated by `metadata.dataOrigin`. The 30-day window is anchored to the first grant, so collection continues indefinitely after onboarding. Phones without a wearable will hold little or no sleep data.

### UsageStatsManager rebuilds exact app intervals, for ten days

The user grants "Usage access" (`PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS`) by toggling an app-op in Settings; there is no runtime dialog ([UsageStatsManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/usage/UsageStatsManager); [Manifest.permission](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/Manifest.permission)). With it, `queryEvents()` returns timestamped `ACTIVITY_RESUMED` and `ACTIVITY_PAUSED` events for each activity, plus screen-interactive and keyguard events and device shutdown and startup markers ([UsageEvents.Event](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/usage/UsageEvents.Event)). AOSP's source applies **no package-visibility filtering to activity events** ([AOSP UsageStatsService](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/main/services/usage/java/com/android/server/usage/UsageStatsService.java)).

Raw events live in daily files **kept for 10 days**. After that only aggregates remain: weekly for 4 weeks, monthly for 6 months and yearly for 2 years ([AOSP UsageStatsDatabase](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aosp-mirror/platform_frameworks_base/main/services/usage/java/com/android/server/usage/UsageStatsDatabase.java)). Since Android 11, the queries return null if the device user is not unlocked ([UsageStatsManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/usage/UsageStatsManager)). The new `queryAppUsageDuration()` in API 37.2 is guarded by a role-only permission, so ordinary apps cannot use it ([Manifest.permission](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/Manifest.permission)).

Searches found **no dedicated Play policy or declaration form for usage access**. The general rules require in-context requests and data use that "the user reasonably expects" ([Play: User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en) [snippet]), and screen-time apps such as ActionDash ship with the permission ([Exodus Privacy: ActionDash](https://reports.exodus-privacy.eu.org/en/reports/85001)). No maintained React Native library exists; every candidate was last published in 2024 or earlier ([npm: react-native-usage-stats](https://registry.npmjs.org/react-native-usage-stats)).

**Inference.** This is Android's strongest unique source, and the mirror image of the iOS gap. On the day the user grants access, LifeTime can backfill **about ten days of exact per-app intervals**, a strong onboarding moment.

The algorithm pairs resumed and paused events per package, closes open intervals at screen-off or keyguard, drops intervals left open across a shutdown, and maps apps to categories with a table (video and games to Entertainment, messaging to Social). LifeTime must poll at least every few days, on every open and through a daily background task, or detail decays into aggregates. Long overnight screen-off spans also give phone-only users a low-confidence sleep proxy.

Play risk looks moderate but could not be confirmed from Google's own text. Three measures address it: a prominent in-app disclosure before sending the user to Settings, on-device storage, and an accurate "App activity" entry in Data safety. The work is a custom Kotlin module of about a day.

### Activity transitions and geofences record only from the moment they are armed

**Activity transitions.** The Activity Recognition Transition API delivers ENTER and EXIT events for IN_VEHICLE, ON_BICYCLE, RUNNING, STILL and WALKING through a PendingIntent, and "the latency of event detection might vary by device" ([Activity Recognition Transition API](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/transitions)). It has needed the runtime `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` permission since Android 10 ([Android 10 privacy changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/10/privacy/changes)). On Android 15, a Force Stop cancels all pending intents ([Android 15 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-all)). There is **no history**. The Sleep API's codelab is now marked deprecated ([Android Sleep API codelab](https://developer.android.com/codelabs/android-sleep-api)).

**Geofences.** An app can register up to 100 geofences, with a minimum radius of 100–150 m. Latency is usually under two minutes and up to about six when the device has been stationary, and geofences must be re-registered after a reboot ([Create and monitor geofences](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/geofencing)).

Background location must be enabled on a settings page on Android 11+ ([Request background location](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/permissions/background)). Play requires a declaration and may reject apps without "strong justification" ([Play policy preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16909972?hl=en) [snippet]). From **27 January 2027**, geofencing stops being an approved foreground-service use case, and apps targeting API 37 must use the location button for one-time precise requests ([Upcoming Android and Google Play deadlines](https://developer.android.com/distribute/play-policies)).

Android has no visit API. Google Maps Timeline moved on-device and has no third-party API, only a user-initiated export ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-google-maps-location-history-3491255/) [snippet]).

**Inference.** On install day, Android cannot reconstruct last week's commute or places at all. The Transition API covers commutes from then on. Geofences on places the user names are the visit substitute, but background location is the hardest review in this whole inventory, so **Android v1 should launch without it** and add it once the core is approved. A Timeline export, imported through the document picker, is a possible later opt-in whose format was not verified.

### Wellbeing, Focus history and notifications stay closed or too broad

There is no public API for Digital Wellbeing data. Bedtime and Focus modes are `AutomaticZenRule`s; an app sees only its own rules plus the global interruption filter ([NotificationManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/NotificationManager)).

A notification listener can read active media sessions, but at the price of seeing every notification ([MediaSessionManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/session/MediaSessionManager)). Android 15 also redacts one-time-password notifications for untrusted listeners ([Android 15 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-all)).

`CalendarContract` is unchanged, and its `Instances` table expands recurring events into start and end times ([CalendarContract](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/provider/CalendarContract)).

**Inference.** Do Not Disturb on and off is at best a weak sleep proxy. The notification listener is too broad an access to justify for a privacy-first time tracker. For media listening time, the music app's foreground-service events in usage stats are a cheaper proxy, though unverified across OEMs.

| Health Connect exercise | Interval, ~60 types, laps, segments | Same | Same | Same | Same | High: Exercise |
| Health Connect mindfulness | Interval with type | Same | Feature-flagged | Same | Same | Low–medium: Self-care |

## Without the calendar, half a week gets a name and four categories stay dark

LifeTime's report sorts time into eight categories: Rest, Nutrition, Exercise, Work, Social, Self-care, Entertainment and Chores ([categories.ts](../src/domain/categories.ts)). The owner fears automatic sources are too thin to fill them. The research supports a precise version of that fear. The sources describe *where* the phone was and *how the body moved or slept* far better than *what the person was doing*. No study measures this coverage, so the quantification below is inference built from the capabilities established above. The one exception is the install-day table, which restates sourced facts.

| On install day, LifeTime can backfill… | iOS | Android |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep, workouts, mindfulness | Full history, or a user-limited window on iOS 27 (length undocumented; 30 days reported) | 30 days before the first grant; more only with READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY |
| Walks, rides, drives | 7 days (Core Motion) | Nothing: the Transition API is live only |
| Places | Nothing: visits and geofences start at registration | Nothing: geofences start at registration |
| Per-app usage | Nothing outside the EU; EU history depth undocumented | About 10 days of exact events, plus weekly and monthly aggregates |
| Calendar events | Everything EventKit holds | Everything CalendarContract holds |

**Inference — a worked week.** Take an office worker and assume these hours, which are assumptions rather than data. They spend 40 hours at the office (lunch included), 5 hours commuting, 3 hours on watch-recorded workouts and 52.5 hours asleep (7.5 hours a night). Another 12 hours are spent out elsewhere (restaurants, friends, shops), and 55.5 hours awake at home, 14 of them on the phone. Assume also that every permission is granted, home and office are named once, and there is no calendar and no Shortcuts setup.

| Block (assumed hours) | iPhone + Apple Watch | iPhone only | Android + wearable, no background location | Android + wearable + geofences | Android phone only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sleep (52.5) | Rest, from HealthKit stages | Rest only if time-in-bed tracking or a Sleep Focus filter is set up | Rest, from a Health Connect session | Rest | Guess from the overnight screen-off gap |
| Office (40) | Work, from a visit at a named place | Work, from a visit | Invisible | Work, from a geofence | Invisible |
| Commute (5) | Bounded, unlabelled (Core Motion) | Bounded, unlabelled | Bounded, unlabelled (transitions) | Bounded, unlabelled | Bounded, unlabelled |
| Workouts (3) | Exercise, from HKWorkout | Walking or running intervals unless recorded in an app | Exercise, from an exercise session | Exercise | Walking or running transitions |
| Out elsewhere (12) | Bounded by visits, unlabelled | Bounded, unlabelled | Invisible | Invisible (not a named geofence) | Invisible |
| Phone time at home (14) | "At home", unlabelled (EU: labelled per hour) | Same | Entertainment or Social, from exact app intervals | Same | Same |
| Rest of home time (41.5) | "At home", unlabelled | Same | Invisible | "At home", unlabelled | Invisible |
| **Confidently labelled** | **95.5 h (57%)**; about 65% in the EU | **40 h (24%)**; about 55% with sleep tracking | **69.5 h (41%)** | **109.5 h (65%)** | **14 h (8%)**; about 40% counting the sleep guess |

Read across, automatic sources alone confidently name between a twelfth and two-thirds of a week, depending far more on hardware and permissions than on the platform. The labels concentrate in Rest, Exercise, office Work and, on Android or in the EU, phone Entertainment. **Nutrition, Social, Self-care and Chores receive essentially nothing** beyond the odd mindfulness session, which leaves four of the eight categories empty.

The result is sensitive to the persona. A remote worker's office hours turn into "home", which drops the iPhone-with-Watch case to 55.5 hours (33%). An iPhone user without a Watch who doesn't track sleep stays at 24% even with perfect location.

On iOS, the largest share of the week lands in a state LifeTime's model does not yet have: **bounded but unlabelled**. In the Watch case that is 72.5 hours (43%) stamped by place with no category. Those labels have to come from the user, or from a rule such as "Gym → Exercise" or "Parents' home → Social", so cheap correction matters more than additional sensors. Commuting fits none of the eight categories either: motion data will produce hours with nowhere to go, so LifeTime must either add a Transport category or fold commutes into Uncategorized.

The day-one asymmetry shapes onboarding. An iPhone can show last week's sleep, workouts and movements, but not places or apps. An Android phone can show last week's sleep, workouts and exact app usage, but not places or movements. Neither can show places until a week after install. Both platforms also impose a cadence. LifeTime must open or wake at least every 7 days on iOS (Core Motion) and every 10 days on Android (usage events), or detail is lost for good.

The owner's fear is therefore justified, but in a narrower form than "too thin". Automatic sources describe the body (sleep and exercise), the office and, on Android, the phone. They do not describe meals, friends, housework or self-care. The workable framing is that **sensors provide the skeleton and the calendar provides the meaning**. The weekly report should show tracked against untracked time openly, extending the minutes-based coverage figure LifeTime already computes ([CATEGORISATION.md](../docs/CATEGORISATION.md)).

The same limits shape future recommendations against goals. With Health Connect's 30-day default and iOS 27's limited-history option (reportedly 30 days), the first recommendations may rest on as little as a month of health data. They should touch only categories with real coverage. Exist.io's practice of withholding insights until "a few weeks" of data exist, and showing their confidence ([Exist manual tracking help](https://exist.io/page/help-manual-tracking/) [snippet]), is the right precedent.

## Only two cloud sources pass the "large, broad and serverless" test

Every fact in this section comes from search-engine extracts of vendor pages, because developers.google.com, strava.com and cloud.ouraring.com could not be fetched. Treat each one as **[snippet]** unless it is marked as secondary or anecdotal.

**Google Calendar** works for native apps without a server. Google treats installed apps as public clients that "cannot keep secrets" and supports PKCE for them ([Google: OAuth 2.0 for iOS & Desktop Apps](https://developers.google.com/accounts/docs/OAuth2InstalledApp)), and it offers free/busy-only scopes next to the read scopes ([Google Calendar API auth scopes](https://developers.google.com/calendar/api/auth)). The read scopes are Sensitive, which means app verification but no CASA security assessment ([Nylas verification guide](https://developer.nylas.com/docs/provider-guides/google/google-verification-security-assessment-guide/), secondary). Until the app is verified, refresh tokens expire after **7 days** ([Google Health API setup](https://developers.google.com/health/setup)), and a secondary source adds a 100-test-user cap ([Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/google-oauth-refresh-token/), secondary). In the browser, Google Identity Services' token model returns no refresh token, so the app must request each new token from a user gesture ([Google GIS migration guide](https://developers.google.com/identity/oauth2/web/guides/migration-to-gis?hl=es-419)). One 2025 blog post also reports that the "Web application" client type still requires a secret for code-plus-PKCE ([ktaka blog](https://ktaka.blog.ccmp.jp/2025/07/oogle-oauth2-and-pkce-understanding.html), single unofficial source).

**Microsoft Graph's** official single-page tutorial uses authorization code with PKCE through MSAL.js 2.0, with no client secret, for personal and work accounts alike ([Microsoft Entra SPA tutorial](https://learn.microsoft.com/entra/identity-platform/tutorial-v2-javascript-auth-code)). Users often cannot consent to newly registered multitenant apps that are not publisher-verified ([Microsoft: Publisher verification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ca/entra/identity-platform/publisher-verification-overview)). One developer reports that verification requires a work account in an Entra tenant ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5829167/cannot-enroll-in-mcpp-%28microsoft-ai-cloud-partner%29), anecdotal).

The wearable and activity clouds have mostly closed to backend-less apps. Oura documents a client-side token flow ([Oura API authentication](https://api.ouraring.com/docs/authentication)), but Gen3 users without an active membership can no longer reach their data through the API ([Oura support: The Oura API](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/4415266939155-The-Oura-API)). Strava requires a `client_secret` for the token exchange ([Strava authentication](https://developers.strava.com/docs/authentication/)), starts new apps with room for a single athlete ([Strava developer program](https://communityhub.strava.com/t5/developer-knowledge-base/our-developer-program/ta-p/8849)), and since November 2024 lets apps show data only to its owner and bans AI use ([Cyclingnews](https://www.cyclingnews.com/news/strava-plan-to-restrict-third-party-apps-has-users-in-an-uproar)). Garmin paused new applications in spring 2026 and reopened on 21 October 2026, but its free Starter tier is **push-only**, which needs a server ([iDevice](https://idevice.com/garmin-reopens-its-connect-api-with-free-personal-keys-and-a-fee-for-big-apps)). Fitbit's legacy Web API was scheduled to end on 30 September 2026 ([dev.fitbit.com](https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/friends/)). Every scope of its successor, the Google Health API, is Restricted ([Google Health API: About](https://developers.google.com/health/about)), and a secondary source says Google is not onboarding new projects ([Sahha](https://sahha.ai/blog/fitbit-api-sunset-migration/), unconfirmed). WHOOP issues a client secret ([Terra](https://tryterra.co/blog/whoop-api-data-access-permissions-limitations-2026), secondary), and Withings is self-serve but signature-based ([Withings developer reference](https://developer.withings.com/llms.md)). Among the non-health sources, Spotify returns only the last 50 plays as timestamps ([Spotify community](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/Get-all-recently-played-tracks-for-a-given-period/td-p/5860931)) and caps development-mode apps at 5 users ([Spotify February 2026 update](https://community.spotify.com/t5/Spotify-for-Developers/February-2026-Spotify-for-Developers-update-thread/m-p/7362921/highlight/true)). RescueTime's OAuth needs manual approval ([RescueTime developers](https://www.rescuetime.com/developers)), Toggl's free plan allows 30 API requests an hour ([Toggl: API limits](https://support.toggl.com/api-webhook-limits)), and GitHub's activity feed holds at most 300 point events from the past 90 days ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-server@3.0/rest/reference/activity)).

| Cloud source | Intervals? | Works without a LifeTime server? | Breadth | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Calendar | Yes | Yes on native (PKCE); on web, one-hour tokens and no background sync | Very broad | Adopt for the web demo and off-phone calendars |
| Microsoft Graph | Yes | Yes (MSAL PKCE) | Broad, work-heavy | Adopt second; publisher verification needed |
| Oura | Yes | Yes, with periodic re-authorization | Niche, needs a membership | Optional, low priority |
| Strava, Garmin, Fitbit/Google Health, WHOOP, Withings | Yes | No or doubtful (secret, push or restricted scopes) | Niche or closing | Skip; read workouts from the health stores |
| Spotify, GitHub | Points only | Barely | Niche | Skip |
| RescueTime, Toggl | Buckets or entries | Only via a pasted personal token | Niche | Skip unless users ask |

**Inference.** On native platforms, cloud calendars add little. Android's CalendarContract already reads synced Google, Exchange and Samsung calendars, and iOS EventKit presumably reads the accounts configured in the Calendar app. Google Calendar and Graph matter mainly for the web demo, which has no device calendar, and for users who keep a work calendar off their phone.

On the web, the token model means sync happens on demand with no background updates, which is acceptable for a demo. Free/busy scopes could serve as a "minimal consent" option that gives intervals without titles, but categorisation needs titles.

The owner's judgement that Strava is niche holds, and Strava is also infeasible without a server. The broader 2024–2026 trend is that vendors gate their APIs with fees, AI-use bans and server requirements while their own apps keep writing workouts and sleep into HealthKit and Health Connect. That makes on-device ingestion both more private and more durable than any wearable cloud. The AI clauses (Strava's ban, Garmin's training ban) would also complicate running even an on-device model over vendor data, one more reason to read through the health stores.

## Apple's on-device model can sort titles today; Android needs a floor

**Apple.** The Foundation Models framework ships from iOS 26 ([Apple: Foundation Models](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels)). Its on-device model is AFM Core (3B parameters) on iPhone 15 Pro and every iPhone 16 and 17. On iOS 27, AFM Core Advanced (20B parameters, 8K context) runs on iPhone Air, iPhone 17 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro, and apps cannot choose the variant ([Apple: Detecting available Apple Foundation Models](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/detecting-available-apple-foundation-models)). In iOS 27, "Apple Intelligence is on by default and can no longer be turned off" (same page). The model is multilingual: `supportsLocale()` checks support, and Apple recommends opening the instructions with "The person's locale is …" ([Apple: Supporting languages and locales](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/supporting-languages-and-locales-with-foundation-models)). French has been an Apple Intelligence language since 2025 ([iPhoneSoft](https://iphonesoft.fr/2025/01/31/tim-cook-confirme-8-nouvelles-langues-apple-intelligence-avril-francais) [snippet]).

The context window is **4,096 tokens per session** ([Apple: Managing the context window](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/managing-the-context-window)). Guided generation with a `@Generable` enum constrains the output "to a set of predefined options" ([Apple: Improving the safety of generative model output](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/improving-the-safety-of-generative-model-output)). On-device use is unlimited and works offline. Private Cloud Compute needs a separate class and a managed entitlement ([Apple: Adding server-side intelligence with PCC](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute)), and without that entitlement the app "crashes with a fatal error" on its first PCC prompt ([Apple: Detecting available Apple Foundation Models](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/detecting-available-apple-foundation-models)).

Guardrails can throw `guardrailViolation` or `refusal`, and the permissive mode does not apply to non-String output ([Apple: permissiveContentTransformations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel/guardrails/permissivecontenttransformations)); Apple says iOS 26.4 and iOS 27 reduced false positives ([WWDC26 session 241](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/)). Rate limits apply only in the background on iOS 26 ([Apple: GenerationError.rateLimited](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelsession/generationerror/ratelimited(_:))), and iOS 27's wording is broader ([Apple: LanguageModelError.rateLimited](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/languagemodelerror/ratelimited(_:))). Apple's only official speed figure dates from 2024, about 30 tokens per second on an iPhone 15 Pro ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models) [snippet]). iOS 27 also lets custom local models run through the same session API via Core AI, which Apple suggests as the fallback for devices without AFM ([WWDC26 session 339](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/339/)).

**Android.** Gemini Nano runs through the ML Kit Prompt API on AICore, which manages the model and leaves the APK untouched ([Android Developers: Gemini Nano](https://developer.android.com/ai/gemini-nano)). It is still beta: `genai-prompt` was at 1.0.0-beta4 in July 2026, when structured output arrived ([ML Kit release notes](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/release-notes) [snippet]). Supported devices are mostly flagships: Pixel 9, 10 and 11, Galaxy Z Flip8 and Fold8, and some Honor, Motorola, OnePlus, OPPO and POCO models ([ML Kit GenAI overview](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai) [snippet]). Input must stay under about 4,000 tokens ([Prompt API get started](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/prompt/android/get-started) [snippet]). AICore enforces a per-app quota (BUSY) and blocks background use, even from a foreground service ([GenAiException.ErrorCode](https://developers.google.com/android/reference/com/google/mlkit/genai/common/GenAiException.ErrorCode) [snippet]). One developer measured failures after **36–41 consecutive generations**, which pacing requests about a second apart avoided ([googlesamples/mlkit #1070](https://github.com/googlesamples/mlkit/issues/1070)). Gemini Nano 4 promises up to 3× faster inference with 4× less memory, alongside a coming alpha Embeddings API based on EmbeddingGemma 2 ([Android Developers Blog, ADS 2026](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/android-intelligence-system-in-app-agents-ads)). Bundling a model instead is heavy: Gemma 4 E2B in LiteRT-LM format weighs 2,583 MB ([LiteRT-LM: Gemma 4](https://ai.google.dev/edge/litert-lm/models/gemma-4) [snippet]).

**Bindings.** Expo now has a first-party module, **`expo-ai` 0.2.0** (3 November 2026). It wraps both system models behind `categorizeAsync(text, { categories })`. It is alpha, needs Xcode 26.4+ and a development build, rejects tasks on Android when the app goes to the background, and states that "native failures and refusals do not trigger a weaker fallback" ([npm: expo-ai](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-ai); [expo-ai docs](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/main/docs/pages/versions/unversioned/sdk/ai.mdx)). Its README calls it unpublished, which contradicts the npm listing.

Callstack's `@react-native-ai/apple` is at 0.12.0 (January 2026), and its Android package was not on npm ([npm: @react-native-ai/apple](https://registry.npmjs.org/@react-native-ai/apple); [callstackincubator/ai](https://github.com/callstackincubator/ai)). `react-native-executorch` 0.11.0 runs bundled models but sends download telemetry unless it is disabled ([GitHub: react-native-executorch](https://github.com/software-mansion/react-native-executorch)).

**Fallback and evidence.** For the fallback, iOS has `NLEmbedding.sentenceEmbedding(for:)` since iOS 14 ([Apple: sentenceEmbedding](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/naturallanguage/nlembedding/sentenceembedding(for:))), with 512-dimensional vectors covering French and English among seven languages ([WWDC20 session 10657](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10657/)). There is no published evaluation of AFM or Nano on short-text classification. A 2026 benchmark puts zero-shot LLMs at a macro F1 of up to 0.67, strongest on topic labels ([BTZSC, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11991) [snippet]).

**Inference.** The "magic wand" designed in [CATEGORISATION.md](../docs/CATEGORISATION.md) is buildable now on iPhone 15 Pro and later, but two details of that design need revising. Its rule "One session, all uncategorised titles, one pass" collides with the 4,096-token window: a few hundred titles need batches of about 20–40 per fresh session. Its note that no Expo module exposes these models is outdated, although `expo-ai`'s one-call-per-title design and alpha status still argue for a thin LifeTime-owned native module that controls batching, pacing and guardrail bisection. Extrapolating Apple's 2024 throughput, 300 uncached titles take one to two minutes, which is acceptable if results stream into the list. On Android the wand is opportunistic: foreground only, paced, and on flagships only.

The real floor everywhere is a cascade. It tries the cache first, then the FR/EN keyword table, then the LLM where one is available, then nearest-neighbour matching on sentence embeddings against shipped examples plus the user's confirmed labels, and otherwise leaves the title "Uncategorized". Two hard controls protect privacy: never add the PCC entitlement (a CI check on the entitlements file costs nothing), and never ship a hybrid or cloud SDK.

Most automatic sources arrive already labelled, with workout types, sleep stages, app bundle IDs and Focus names. They need deterministic mapping tables, not a model. The LLM's job stays free text: calendar titles, the names users give places, and unfamiliar app names. For later recommendations, a week summarised as minutes per category against goals fits easily in 4K tokens. The goal arithmetic should stay deterministic, as it already is in LifeTime's goals domain, and the model should at most phrase the advice.

## Competitors log places or screens, and none merges all five signals

Every competitor fact here comes from search extracts of store listings, vendor pages and reviews **[snippet]**.

Existing products fall into a location-and-motion family and a screen-activity family. In the first, Life Cycle tracks passively from cell towers and Wi-Fi rather than background GPS, claims under 1% battery a day, and reads Sleep Analysis and Steps from Apple Health ([App Store: Life Cycle](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id1064955217)). Its reviewers complain that it "constantly fails to notice that I've left home" and that places appear days late ([AppFollow review mirror](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/life-cycle-track-your-time/1064955217?country=ca), anecdotal). Arc Timeline is built on LocoKit, which models a day as Visits and Paths and tells car, train, bus and plane apart ([LocoKit](https://cocoapods.org/pods/LocoKit)), though whether Arc is still maintained is disputed ([Dawarich vs Arc](https://dawarich.app/docs/comparisons/vs-arc/)). Google Maps Timeline moved on-device, migrating only up to 90 days of data ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/google-could-delete-your-google-maps-timeline-data-in-the-name-of-privacy-so-take-action-now/?mid=1)), and users report losing years of history when changing phones ([r/GoogleMaps megathread](https://reddit.fabiomanganiello.com/r/GoogleMaps/comments/1diivt3/megathread_google_maps_timeline_moving_to), anecdotal).

In the screen-activity family, Timing (macOS) keeps automatic app usage separate from confirmed time entries, suggests "blocks of time that belong together", and claims that a few rules "reduce … manual categorization by 80–90%" ([Timing quick start](https://timingapp.com/help/quick-start)). Rize uses AI to assign time to projects from window titles and URLs ([Makerstack review](https://makerstack.co/reviews/rize-review/)). RescueTime's aggregated complaints include privacy concerns ([CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/time-tracking/rescuetime/)). Dayflow records the screen and lets a local or cloud LLM write the timeline ([GitHub: Dayflow](https://github.com/JerryZLiu/Dayflow)). **Exist.io** correlates daily totals, waits weeks before showing insights, and lets users rate and hide them ([Exist blog](https://exist.io/blog/new-ios-correlations/)). The newest entrants sell privacy first: Replay records places and photos local-first ([Product Hunt: Replay](https://www.producthunt.com/products/replay-ios-app/launches/replay-life-timeline)), Time Atlas offers optional end-to-end encrypted sync ([App Store: Time Atlas](https://apps.apple.com/app/time-atlas/id6738783076)), and TimeLoc promises "no cloud, no account" ([Product Hunt: TimeLoc](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/timeloc-private-location-timeline)).

**Inference.** No product found merges calendar, sleep, workouts, location visits and screen time into one deduplicated interval timeline. Life Cycle comes closest, with places plus Health sleep. That gap is LifeTime's opening and its hardest engineering.

Three lessons transfer. **Missed or late location detection is the main reputational risk**: it is inherent to low-power designs, and LifeTime's CLVisit path shares it, so correction must be cheap. **Rules learned from corrections do most of the work** in the best-reviewed tool, which makes LifeTime's rules engine the right base. And **on-device storage needs export and backup**, or LifeTime will repeat Google Timeline's data-loss complaints. On pricing, Life Cycle's premium tiers run from about €5 to €36 on the French store ([App Store FR: Life Cycle](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/id1064955217)) and Gyroscope's from $39 to $299 ([App Store: Gyroscope](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1104085053)). Desktop trackers such as RescueTime and Rize charge roughly $7–15 a month ([CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/time-tracking/rescuetime/); [Makerstack review](https://makerstack.co/reviews/rize-review/)), and subscription cost is a recurring complaint.

## Merge immutable observations into a separate, correctable timeline

No competitor documents its conflict-resolution logic, so this section is design inference anchored to documented behaviour. Timing's separation of raw usage from confirmed entries is the only published model ([Timing quick start](https://timingapp.com/help/quick-start) [snippet]).

**Inference — two layers.** The design has two layers. Every sleep session, workout, visit, motion interval, app interval, Focus interval and calendar event is stored as an immutable *observation* with its source, provenance and confidence. LifeTime already ships `expo-sqlite` ([package.json](../package.json)), so this can be a local table. A separate *timeline* of resolved blocks is derived from the observations, and user corrections write to it as rules or priors, never as edits to raw data. With this split, revoking a source deletes exactly that source's observations. The timeline resolves overlaps by precedence:

| Rank | Evidence | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | User entries and corrections | Always win; become rules |
| 2 | Device-measured bounded activities: workouts, sleep, mindfulness | Exact label and edges |
| 3 | Place visits and geofences; motion intervals | Edges and place; category via place rules |
| 4 | Calendar events | Plans: "planned" until corroborated; accepted, timed events strong; all-day, tentative or "free" ones weak |
| 5 | App-usage intervals (Android) and hourly Screen Time buckets | Label phone time; never override a higher-ranked activity |
| 6 | Points: completed reminders, photos, State of Mind | Corroborate or name a block, never create one |

Nested intervals should refine rather than conflict. A workout inside a gym visit, or a meeting inside an office visit, lets the more specific child win while the parent fills the remainder. Same-type intervals from several writers (the same run arriving from Garmin and Strava through the health store) merge when they overlap heavily, with Health Connect's `dataOrigin` and HealthKit's source attribution choosing the canonical copy. The calendar becomes an audit. A visit at the event's place, or a quiet phone during the slot, upgrades a plan to "likely". A contradiction, such as being at home during an "office" meeting or a workout overlapping it, flags the block for review instead of silently picking a winner.

The importers must also normalise each platform's documented quirks. HealthKit stage samples overlap the in-bed sample but not each other, and Apple Watch omits awake samples at the edges of a night ([Apple: HKCategoryValueSleepAnalysis](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkcategoryvaluesleepanalysis)). Health Connect stages are sequential and non-overlapping, with gaps allowed ([Health Connect: Track sleep sessions](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/features/sleep-sessions)). A missing visit arrival is `distantPast`, and a still-open visit has a `distantFuture` departure ([Apple: CLVisit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corelocation/clvisit)). Core Motion flags coexist ([Apple: CMMotionActivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionactivity)). Open usage events across a shutdown "should be ignored because the closing time is unknown" ([UsageEvents.Event](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/usage/UsageEvents.Event)).

**Inference — visible confidence.** Each block should show one of five states: confirmed, likely, guessed, located-but-unlabelled, or unaccounted. Unaccounted time should be a first-class slice that invites the user to fill the largest gap first, not something quietly attributed to "Home". This slots into the existing design. `src/domain/rules.ts` already resolves categories by specificity, and `ResolutionSource` already reserves `'model'` ([CATEGORISATION.md](../docs/CATEGORISATION.md)). Place, app and health sources become further resolution sources, and every "always do this" tap becomes a rule. Counting rule hits yields an honest "X% of your week categorised automatically" figure, the measure Timing markets.

## Expo SDK 58 clears the Xcode 27 wall; four sensors still need custom code

### Scene adoption gates every iOS 27 build

LifeTime runs Expo `~57.0.11` with React Native 0.86.2 ([package.json](../package.json)). It pins Xcode 26.6 because Xcode 27's SDK makes the UIScene life cycle mandatory ([RELEASE.md](../docs/RELEASE.md)), and Apple says apps built with the latest SDK must adopt scenes "or they fail to launch" ([Apple: Updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates)).

Expo's latest release, **`expo` 58.0.23** (3 November 2026), pins React Native 0.88.1 and React 19.3.0 ([npm: expo](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo); [expo 58.0.23 bundledNativeModules](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo/-/expo-58.0.23.tgz)). Its prebuild template adds a `SceneDelegate` subclassing `ExpoAppSceneDelegate` ([template 58.0.32](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-template-bare-minimum/-/expo-template-bare-minimum-58.0.32.tgz)). The header of that class says iOS 27 "asserts at launch unless the app adopts the scene-based life cycle" ([expo 58.0.23 source](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo/-/expo-58.0.23.tgz)). There is also a backport for SDK 57: `expo-build-properties` `ios.enableSceneSupport`, which requires expo 57.0.23 or newer and supports "only the standard SDK 57 Swift AppDelegate template" ([expo-build-properties 57.0.23](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-build-properties/-/expo-build-properties-57.0.23.tgz)).

Xcode 27 brings other changes. It replaces Simulator.app with DeviceHub, which broke `expo run:ios` in one project ([drafto PR #774](https://github.com/JakubAnderwald/drafto/pull/774)) and which `@expo/cli` 58.1.19 and 57.0.33 now handle ([@expo/cli 58.1.19](https://registry.npmjs.org/@expo/cli/-/cli-58.1.19.tgz)). iOS 27 makes iPhone apps resizable, and orientation locks may stop applying ([expo-screen-orientation v58 docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/screen-orientation/) [snippet]). Config plugins that patch the AppDelegate are the likeliest to break ([Customer.io: iOS 27 UIScene support](https://docs.customer.io/integrations/sdk/expo/whats-new/ios-27-uiscene-support/) [snippet]).

**Inference.** There are two exits from the 26.6 pin. The backport is the smaller change, because all of LifeTime's config plugins are first-party. The full SDK 58 upgrade moves React Native to 0.88. Either should come before any sensor library is added, so that each new native dependency is proven with one prebuild-and-launch on Xcode 27. `ExpoAppSceneDelegate` forwards user-activity and URL events, the path that App Intents and Shortcuts invocations take.

### Libraries exist for health, intents and widgets; the rest is custom Swift and Kotlin

The table draws on [npm: @kingstinct/react-native-healthkit](https://registry.npmjs.org/@kingstinct/react-native-healthkit), [npm: react-native-health-connect](https://registry.npmjs.org/react-native-health-connect), [expo-app-intents](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-app-intents/-/expo-app-intents-0.5.16.tgz), [npm: expo-widgets](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-widgets), [npm: react-native-device-activity](https://registry.npmjs.org/react-native-device-activity), [GitHub: expo-apple-targets](https://github.com/EvanBacon/expo-apple-targets), [expo-location 58.0.20](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-location/-/expo-location-58.0.20.tgz), [npm: expo-background-task](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-background-task) and [npm: expo-ai](https://registry.npmjs.org/expo-ai). Local modules are scaffolded with `create-expo-module` 58.0.3 ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/create-expo-module)). Code that must sit in the app target uses `@expo/inline-modules` 0.2.9, the pattern `expo-app-intents` itself relies on ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@expo/inline-modules)).

**Inference.** A single local module, for example `modules/lifetime-sensors`, can host the four missing wrappers: visits, motion history, usage events and activity transitions. Each is roughly 100–250 lines of Swift or Kotlin plus a small config plugin, about a day including the permission flow. The Screen Time report extension is the costly exception: it takes several days, runs in a separate process, is testable only on a device, and is blocked on Apple's approval. All of this keeps `expo prebuild` in CI with no committed native folders. Calendar access is unchanged, since `expo-calendar`, which LifeTime already uses, wraps both EventKit and CalendarContract.

### Native screenshots can come from CI, but sensor data cannot

LifeTime's `diagnose-ios.yml` already prebuilds, builds an unsigned Release app for the simulator, and launches it in about 16 minutes ([diagnose-ios.yml](../.github/workflows/diagnose-ios.yml)). The tooling around it has caught up with Xcode 27. Maestro 2.12.1 shipped on 2 November ([Maestro releases](https://github.com/mobile-dev-inc/maestro/releases)). Version 2.12.0 starts simulators on Xcode 27 without Simulator.app, and 2.11.0 added Android 17 ([Maestro changelog](https://github.com/mobile-dev-inc/maestro/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md)). GitHub's runner list shows `xcode-27` arm64 labels with a "Preview" badge ([actions/runner-images](https://github.com/actions/runner-images)), while GitHub's changelog announced them as generally available on 15 October 2026 ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-15-xcode-27-runner-image-generally-available) [snippet]). Android emulators run on Ubuntu with KVM through `reactivecircus/android-emulator-runner` ([GitHub](https://github.com/ReactiveCircus/android-emulator-runner)), and at least one public Expo project already runs prebuild, a simulator build and Maestro on GitHub Actions without EAS ([slovo PR #458](https://github.com/ToxaNn1/slovo/pull/458)).

Expo's own tooling offers alternatives. `eas-cli` 27.5.0 has experimental `eas simulator` sessions with `simulator:control screenshot.capture` ([eas-cli 27.5.0](https://registry.npmjs.org/eas-cli/-/eas-cli-27.5.0.tgz)). `@expo/agent-cli` 1.0.24 adds agent-driven `smoke` and `runtime:*` commands ([npm: @expo/agent-cli](https://registry.npmjs.org/@expo/agent-cli)). EAS Workflows' Maestro job is alpha ([EAS Workflows e2e reference](https://docs.expo.dev/eas/workflows/reference/e2e-tests/) [snippet]). GitHub lists a standard macOS runner at $0.062 per minute ([GitHub runner pricing](https://docs.github.com/en/enterprise-cloud@latest/billing/reference/actions-runner-pricing)). The simulator keeps HealthKit data an app saves ([Apple: Accessing sample data in the simulator](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/accessing-sample-data-in-the-simulator)), but background delivery is unsupported there ([Apple: enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))).

**Inference.** The cheapest loop extends `diagnose-ios.yml`. It boots a simulator, grants calendar, location and motion with `simctl privacy`, runs Maestro flows that call `takeScreenshot`, and uploads the PNGs as an artifact that an agent fetches with `gh run download`. A run takes roughly 20–25 minutes, about $1.25–1.55 on a private repository. Use `macos-26` until scenes are adopted, then `xcode-27`, and capture several window sizes now that iOS 27 apps are resizable.

The limit is data, not pixels. Visits, motion history, Screen Time and usage stats produce nothing in a simulator, and the HealthKit permission sheet must be tapped through. Each sensor module therefore needs a debug-only fixture path: a seed deep link honoured only under `__DEV__` or a launch argument. It can reuse the deterministic week LifeTime already generates in `src/domain/demo.ts` ([CATEGORISATION.md](../docs/CATEGORISATION.md)). The same fixtures are the only honest way to show automatic sources in the web demo, which has no sensors at all. Real-world behaviour, such as approximate visit edges, missed departures and threshold misfires, still needs a phone carried in a pocket for a week.

## Conclusion

The research moves the question from "which sources exist" to "which states the timeline must represent". The sensors' main contribution is **boundaries**, not labels. An iPhone can stamp nearly every hour with a place, but it can name only the hours spent asleep, training or at a named office. LifeTime's leverage therefore lies less in adding sensors than in turning "located but unlabelled" time into categories through cheap, learned rules, and its existing specificity-ranked rules engine is already the right shape for that. The platforms also call for different first features rather than strict parity. iOS should lead with HealthKit plus visits. Android should lead with Health Connect plus usage events, and defer background location until the core has passed Play review.

The deeper reframing concerns the calendar. The categories where sensors are blind (Social, Nutrition, Chores, Self-care) are exactly the ones the owner's calendar already describes. The calendar is therefore not a legacy input to replace but the semantic layer, and the sensors are its audit. "You planned three gym sessions; your watch saw one" is a recommendation neither source can make alone, and it needs no cloud, no server and no model. The sequencing follows from policy more than from code. Adopt scenes first. File the Family Controls request months ahead if Screen Time matters for EU users. Write the store copy around health and device-usage management. Then ship each source behind an honest "tracked vs untracked" figure.
